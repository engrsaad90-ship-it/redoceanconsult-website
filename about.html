Who we are

Hands-on construction experience, applied to programmes and claims

Red Ocean Consult was founded in 2019, born out of years spent on live construction sites across Saudi Arabia. Time and again, our founders watched capable contractors carry the cost of delays they had not caused. Late access, delayed approvals, design changes and late instructions from the employer pushed completion dates back, yet the contractor was left to absorb the time, the prolongation costs and, too often, liquidated damages.

The problem was rarely a lack of entitlement. It was a lack of evidence. Programmes were not kept up to date, records were scattered, and delay events were never linked to the critical path in a way an engineer or tribunal could follow.

We set up Red Ocean Consult to change that. We combined hands-on construction experience with forensic scheduling, so that every delay is traced to its cause, every day of extension is supported by records, and every claim can be followed from event to entitlement.

Facts first. Method second. Position third.

To protect the legal rights of contractors for delays caused by other parties, and to bring transparency and efficiency to every table where time and money are decided.

The case for specialist support

Delay is the norm in Saudi construction. Evidence is not.

Contractors rarely lose claims because they were not entitled. They lose them because the programme, records and notices were not there when it mattered.

These are separate studies with different samples and dates, not one market forecast. When delay originates with the employer, an approved programme and records are what turn it into recoverable time and cost.

Hurdle 1

No approved baseline

Nothing agreed to measure delay against. The critical path is contested and EOT claims lose their reference point.

We build it and get it approved

  • Review the contract, scope and milestones
  • Build the WBS and logic in Primavera P6
  • Incorporate consultant comments until approval
  • Reconstruct a reasoned programme where work started without one
Hurdle 2

No contemporaneous records

Events are hard to prove after the fact, claim values are challenged line by line, and concurrency cannot be separated.

We set up and keep the records

  • Monthly programme updates with variance narratives
  • Templates for daily site records and progress
  • A Document Controller to index and time-stamp everything
  • Rebuild the chronology where records are patchy
Hurdle 3

No official letters or notices

Missing or late notices can forfeit time and money under time-bar clauses. Silence can be read as acceptance.

We draft and track the notices

  • Identify notice and time-bar requirements
  • Draft early warning, delay and claim notices
  • Keep a notice register and deadline tracker
  • Link each letter to the event and the programme
